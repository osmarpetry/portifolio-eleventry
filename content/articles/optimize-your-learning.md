# Optimize Your Learning

## Goal
Design study loops that balance planning, execution, and reflection so progress compounds instead of stalling.

## Principles
- Separate theory and practice; study sources at least three times before declaring confusion.
- Choose goals using Bloom's taxonomy levels (remember → create) and build a ladder of steps.
- Use feedback loops: self-testing, mentor reviews, and peer teaching.
- Break goals into "today" vs "later" milestones.
- Mix inputs (reading, watching) with outputs (writing, building).
- Seek fast feedback (peer review, tests, prototypes).
- Track experiments; keep the ones with measurable impact.
- Treat breaks and rest as inputs to learning quality.

## Deliberate Learning Loop
1. **Frame the outcome** — define what "done" looks like. Articulate success criteria and the format (essay, talk, demo).
2. **Choose the method** — resources, mentors, exercises. Break into milestones with tools, constraints, and acceptance tests.
3. **Time-box & execute** — short sprints with focused practice. Consume theory, take structured notes, capture definitions.
4. **Practice** — apply via projects, code katas, or teaching others.
5. **Reflect & adjust** — note what worked, blockers, and next tweak. Log reflections and adjust the plan.

## Tools & Habits
- Spaced repetition (Anki, SuperMemo) for definitions and formulas.
- Project management (Notion, Obsidian, Trello) to track the ladder.
- Version control (Git) for reproducibility; store notes and code.
- Weekly review: what moved the needle? what to change?
- Learning journal: capture insights and questions.
- Accountability partner or stand-up to maintain cadence.
- Time blocking for deep work vs exploration.

## Mindset
- Use high-performance cycle: set commitment, focus, feedback, and adapt to complexity.
- Practice teaching; summarize in your own words to cement knowledge.
- Run weekly retrospectives: wins, gaps, next experiments.
- Seek mentor reviews or code comments.
- Iterate on acceptance criteria when the context shifts.
