# Smarter Faster Better — decision playbook

Charles Duhigg narrows productivity down to smarter choices, not longer workdays. The notes below combine highlights from the book with decision prompts I use before accepting a project or opportunity.

## 📌 Rapid triage when a new proposal arrives
- **Score the upside vs. risk**: assign likelihood/impact percentages for revenue, learning, and reputation; compare against time cost and worst-case exposure.
- **Phone a domain friend**: get a 10-minute gut check from someone with context; look for red flags on culture and incentives.
- **Force a number**: if data is vague, generate a provisional forecast (e.g., 30% chance of achieving X) so the decision is explicit rather than emotional.

## 🧠 Creative friction is a feature
- When a team hits “creative conflict,” treat it as a trigger to widen the solution space — the book shows that dissent often precedes breakthroughs.
- Rotate roles (skeptic, builder, storyteller) each meeting so objections surface early and ideas stay actionable.
- Archive all brainstorm artifacts; revisit contrarian ideas after initial prototypes come back with data.

## 🎓 Learn it, teach it
- Convert every chapter or concept into a short explainer for a peer; teaching locks in understanding more than passive note-taking.
- Prep a one-slide “friend briefing” before demos: problem, operating assumptions, decision deadline.
- Preface and final chapter serve as executive summaries — review them monthly to keep the big ideas sticky.

## 🔁 Weekly review prompts
1. **What decision did I delay because the data felt incomplete?** Rewrite it with a probability range.
2. **Who challenged my idea this week?** If nobody, seek dissent intentionally.
3. **Can I explain the current project in 90 seconds?** If not, rebuild the briefing.

## 📚 Further reading
- Charles Duhigg, *Smarter Faster Better* (Random House, 2016).
- Harvard Business Review, “The Power of Friction in Innovation Teams” (2023 update on constructive dissent).
- MIT Sloan Management Review, “Decision Making in the Age of AI” (framework for probabilistic bets).
