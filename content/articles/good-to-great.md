# Good to Great — leadership & operating system

Jim Collins dissects why a handful of companies broke out from mediocrity. These notes adapt his findings into engineering and product leadership moves.

## 🧭 Level-5 leadership
- Blend humility with hard-nosed ambition for the mission, not your résumé.
- Protect the team from external thrash; take blame publicly and share wins widely.
- Ladder approximation for engineers: apprentice → fixer → owner → coach. Coach the successor before you exit a role.

## 🪑 First Who, Then What
- Hire people who thrive under autonomy and clarity, then co-create the strategy.
- Run “culture fit” by behaviours: curiosity, candour, follow-through. Document disqualifiers (e.g., blame shifting).
- When moving someone out, do it quickly and respectfully; open seats drain momentum.

## 🦔 Hedgehog Concept (3 intersections)
1. **What you can be best at** (unique engineering edge).
2. **What drives your economic engine** (pricing, usage, or retention levers).
3. **What you are deeply passionate about** (mission that keeps the team invested).
- Plot initiatives against the diagram; projects outside the overlap get sunset or handed off.

## 🔁 Flywheel vs. Doom Loop
- **Flywheel**: consistent small wins → trust → more investment → better results. Log the flywheel components (e.g., great DX → faster feature shipping → happier customers → referrals).
- **Doom loop**: reactive strategy hopping. To avoid, audit every pivot for data and runway.

## 💡 Tech accelerators
- Technology is a force multiplier, not a direction setter. Adopt tools that amplify the Hedgehog Concept.
- For product teams: choose platforms that reduce lead time and error rates; track metrics before/after adoption.

## 🧾 Diagnostics checklist
- Do we have the right people in the critical seats?
- Can everyone state the Hedgehog Concept in one sentence?
- What is our flywheel, and which link is weakening?
- Where are we chasing distractions outside the Hedgehog overlap?

## 📚 Resources & case updates

- Stanford GSB: “Level 5 Leadership Revisited” (2022 data on humility-driven CEOs).
- Harvard Business Review: “First Who, Then What — 20 Years Later” (2023 product org examples).
