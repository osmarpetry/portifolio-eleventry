# What Makes a Great Software Engineer

## 📄 Abstract
Meyer, Zimmermann, and Fritz (ICSE 2015) interviewed 59 senior Microsoft engineers to identify 53 characteristics that distinguish great engineers. Traits fall into four domains: personal qualities, decision-making, team impact, and product impact. The study critiques traditional curricula for lacking detail on real-world behaviours.

## 🔑 Key points
- **Methodology**: 59 interviews across 10 Microsoft projects; participants recognized by peers.
- **Personal traits**: continuous improvement, intrinsic motivation, open-mindedness.
- **Decision-making**: understanding organizational context, balancing big picture and details.
- **Team impact**: creating shared context, fostering psychological safety, empathy, honesty.
- **Product impact**: anticipating future needs, balancing generalization vs specificity.
- **Curriculum critique**: ACM/IEEE guidelines seen as prescriptive but insufficiently descriptive of day-to-day practice.
- **Environment**: error-tolerant, experimental culture crucial for innovation and growth.

## 🔗 Connections
- Adds depth to soft skills discussions in software engineering.
- Aligns with research on organizational culture, psychological safety, and motivation.
- Supports literature on technical leadership and human factors in project success.

## ❓ Questions
- How to cultivate these traits under tight deadlines?
- Which traits matter most in startups vs large enterprises?
- How to evaluate soft skills in performance reviews?
- How much does organizational context enable or limit development of these traits?

## 📝 Personal reflections
- Excellence extends beyond technical mastery; self-development, empathy, systems thinking, and collaboration are pivotal.
- Leaders must prioritize culture and psychological safety.
- Training should be more practical and context-aware than traditional curricula suggest.
- Use findings to guide career development and performance evaluation discussions.

## 📚 Detailed notes
- Interviews highlighted gap between academic curricula and practical expectations.
- Characteristics’ relevance varies by company culture, product, and team.
- Limitations: Microsoft-only sample; generalization requires caution.
- Original paper: [https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/papers/Meyer2015GreatEngineers.pdf](https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/papers/Meyer2015GreatEngineers.pdf)
